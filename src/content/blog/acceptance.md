---
title: something to not be named
description: |
  my rambles about space. a beautiful thing, isn't it?
  (cw: existential, death mentioned)
date: 2026-11-02
---
have you once wondered where we, and, in particular, you came from? i think most of us have, if not all of us - but that's a big claim i'm not willing to make.

we may give ourselves a dozen or two explanaitions, some of them we make up to not be afraid of unknowingness, others are made up from what we found and accomplished in the history of human race.

there's one thing we know for sure - that below us, behind the layer we lovingly call "sky", hides the space. what is space? if we want to read the wikipedia answer on this, we'll get that it's an expanse that exists beyond earth's atmosphere and between celestial bodies. but that answer is not something you came here for, is it?

in my more simple version, outer space is the seemingly bottomless pit, filled with various sorts of things, some within, and some beyond our understanding that's embedded into human nature.

and coming back just a few sentences, we're always trying to scratch the inch of not knowing our real origins - what was before the initial singularity, before the big bang? was there something? nothing at all? did a concept of time even exist back then? 

what we know for sure is that we don't know. and this also applies to a lot of different parts of our vast universe - galaxies, countless exoplanets, pulsars, black holes, then the possibility of white holes and wormholes. so many things, and so many lack a simple explanation, simply because there isn't one. because it never intended for itself to be explained by a small group of living organisms that gained sentience.

and, maybe it was not something meant to be marked with words?

we don't know why some things are the way they are - others get explanaition, but not all. we hit the wall, bang our head against it, and repeat. yet we'll never know the real reason of why on some things - and, really, it's fine. 

i know, a lot of people have this manic grasp to know and control everything - for every piece of their lives or for some specific topic, they reap to know everything, the absolute truth of everything and how it really is. in some way, i was once like that - i thought that everything in my life was only my responsibility, only for me to control, then i got very mad and sad when something outside of my grasp has happened that worsened my life quality.

yet, you can't know everything. and you can't control everything either. some of the things are outside of your, or humanity's grasp, and that's okay.

for what we know: there was once an explosion, then the universe begun, then there was this very hot planet that slowly cooled down and gained life on it. from that life, came we. me, writing this, and you, reading this.

and then, there's us. humans. who still argue and get in conficts about where we really came from, even if that wasn't answered countless times before already. who maybe can't decide our favorite color, who maybe are uncertain on which piece of our planet we want to reside. or maybe we argue at the family dinner over something so small, yet so big. maybe you are small in the grand scheme of things, but your own little world inside our massive globe feels like all in the world. as if there's nothing on the outside.

we joke, laugh, then cry, and came back with a smile again to face it all. face our own little worlds, and the big, wide world, who unites us all. we worry about paychecks and what to make for breakfeast tomorrow, or maybe we wait in anticipation for our favorite series to drop a new banger.

there's better places and graves for us to visit, rather than spend all time dwelling over the inevitable.

what we also know, is that we came from stars - stardust is inside of each us, in our bones and blood. and at one point, we'll go back to being stars. and i'm okay with that.

but in the meantime, there's errands to run, things to get done, and people to love. i'll have my time to think about it all, when i'll be on my deathbed - but that time isn't now, and i hope isn't anytime soon.

so, no, we don't know, so many things. and we may never know.

the important thing is to not dwell on the "why?" - it's to accept that it just is, and maybe admire the beauty of it.

perhaps not everything is meant to be named.

i don't know why we are here. i don't know what's the meaning of my life, or what happens after it.

i really don't know, but

i'll take it.
